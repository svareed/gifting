Sales call · Saal Anatolia, Duisburg · 15 minutes

The Anatolia Close

How Davet gets sold for €199 a month to a wedding hall that has never bought software — line by line, with the reasoning beside it.

The man across the table

Not a target segment. One person, with numbers that have to add up on Saturday.

Murat Özdemir, 47 Owner, Saal Anatolia — Duisburg-Marxloh
The business
Two halls, 450 and 220 seats. Took it over from his father in 2011.
Volume
85 events a year — 60 weddings, plus engagements, henna nights, circumcision parties.
One evening
Avg. 380 guests, €48 menu, roughly €18,000 in revenue.
Staff
Six permanent, up to 20 casuals on a Saturday. His wife does the books.
What keeps him up
The headcount arrives three days before the event — and is off by 30 to 60 people every single time.
Marketing
Instagram, 8,200 followers. One wedding fair a year at €2,400 a booth. Otherwise word of mouth.
How he buys
Bought a €4,000 website in 2019 that nobody maintains. Suspicious of "digitalisation" ever since. Pays by invoice.
Decision rule
Does Saturday night get calmer without him having to sit down at a computer? Then yes.

The number, before you drive over

Never calculate with the menu price. A hall owner knows his food cost, will correct you instantly — and then the meeting is over. Calculate with what he pays for the ingredients, and he calculates along with you.

And that is only the kitchen. Sixty weddings times 380 guests is 22,800 people a year opening an invitation with his hall on it. Half a percent of them are planning a wedding themselves — 114 warm leads he gives away for free today. Two bookings out of that is €36,000 in revenue.

The conversation

Friday morning, in the hall, not the office. No laptop on the table. No deck. English above, the words you actually say underneath.

Minute 0

"Herr Özdemir, I sent you something on WhatsApp. Go on, open it."

„Herr Özdemir, ich hab Ihnen was auf WhatsApp geschickt. Machen Sie's mal auf."

Then put your own phone away and say nothing. He opens a finished invitation for a wedding happening at his hall next month — his logo, his hall, his photo, Iznik blue, in Turkish. The veil parts.

Ownership before purchase

He is holding it, on his own device, with his own name on it. From this second on he is not weighing up a product — he is deciding whether to give back something that already feels like his.

Minute 2

Say nothing.

Let him scroll. He will turn the phone around and show someone — the chef, his son, his wife. Wait for that. That is the proof, not whatever you say next.

Salesman talk

In the German Mittelstand, a smooth flow of words is a warning sign. Staying quiet while the product does the work reads as someone who knows their product.

Minute 3

"Four hundred guests get that on their phone. My company's name is nowhere on it. Only your hall."

„Das kriegen 400 Gäste aufs Handy. Meine Firma steht da nirgends drauf. Nur Ihr Saal."

Standing

In owner-run businesses, pride is a harder buying motive than efficiency — and software sellers almost always miss it. His father's hall, presented better than it ever has been.

Minute 4

"Can I ask you something — how many covers do you cook too many, just to be safe?"
… "And what does one cover cost you to buy in?"

„Darf ich Sie was fragen: Wie viele Gedecke kochen Sie sicherheitshalber zu viel?" … „Und was kostet Sie ein Gedeck im Einkauf?"

Write his numbers on paper, not yours. Times sixty nights. Slide the paper across and leave it lying there.

Self-generated

A number he said out loud himself, he cannot dispute. A number from your slide is a claim, and claims get argued with.

Minute 7

"And this is what it looks like for you, three weeks out."

„Und so sieht das für Sie aus, drei Wochen vorher."

One screenshot on the phone: 312 attending · 47 declined · 28 children under 6 · 14 vegetarian. Underneath: "As of nine o'clock this morning."

From worry to number

His problem was never "not enough invitations". It was not knowing, on the Wednesday before the Saturday. Show the uncertainty disappearing — not how pretty the design is.

Minute 9

"You don't have to log in anywhere. You send me the couple's names, the date and which hall, on WhatsApp. The invitation is up the next day."

„Sie müssen sich nirgends einloggen. Sie schicken mir per WhatsApp: Namen des Paares, Datum, welcher Saal. Am nächsten Tag steht die Einladung."

The actual lever

He doesn't want software, he wants an outcome. "We do it for you" beats "you can do it yourself" in owner-run businesses every time — and it keeps you close to the customer through your first year, which is where you learn the product.

Minute 11

"The first three weddings are on me. If the numbers don't hold up after that, you've paid nothing and we shake hands."

„Die ersten drei Hochzeiten mache ich umsonst. Wenn die Zahlen danach nicht stimmen, haben Sie nichts gezahlt und wir geben uns die Hand."

Risk, not upside

German small-business owners buy certainty, not opportunity. An argument that lowers his risk lands harder than one that raises his gain, at the identical amount of money.

Minute 13

"€199 a month. Unlimited weddings. Cancel monthly. Invoice or direct debit, whichever you prefer."

„199 € im Monat. Unbegrenzt viele Hochzeiten. Monatlich kündbar. Rechnung oder Lastschrift, wie Sie möchten."

Four sentences. Then stay silent until he answers — even if it takes twenty seconds.

A price with no leaflet

Explaining a price is apologising for it. "Cancel monthly" takes the sting out of the figure: he isn't deciding on €2,388, he's deciding on €199.

Minute 14

"I got it down to one page. The rest is paperwork, and that's mine to do."

„Ich hab's auf eine Seite gebracht. Der Rest ist Papierkram, den mache ich."

One-page contract, data-processing agreement stapled to it, SEPA mandate. The pen is already on the table.

No second meeting

A proposal he should "look over calmly" dies in a pile. Bringing the data-processing agreement unasked signals: this one has worked with German businesses before.

What he will say

Four objections cover almost everything. Every answer is shorter than the objection.

"My couples want printed cards."„Meine Paare wollen gedruckte Karten."

"Of course. You get the print file in the same design, with a QR code on it. The card stays — the counting goes digital. Your printer keeps earning, you stop guessing."

"What about data protection?"„Und der Datenschutz?"

"Servers in Germany. Data-processing agreement is right here, ready to sign. No Google fonts, no trackers — your guests don't even see a cookie banner. Guest data deletes itself 90 days after the wedding."

"I've already got Instagram."„Ich hab doch Instagram."

"Instagram reaches people who follow you. The invitation reaches 400 guests who just attended a wedding in your hall. There are no better leads than that — and you give them away every Saturday."

"And if you're gone in two years?"„Und wenn es Sie in zwei Jahren nicht mehr gibt?"

"Then you press one button and have everything as PDF and Excel on your computer. It's in the contract. I'd have asked the same thing."

What has to be built before you ring the bell

Without these six, the conversation above is a bluff. With them, it's an appointment.

  • German interface, German domain. The landing page, the login and the theme names exist only in English today — <html lang="en"> is hardcoded in the layout. A hall owner lands on "Wedding invitations, without the web developer" and leaves.
  • Multi-tenancy. The schema knows only owner_id. Without organisations, logos, subdomains and quotas there is no B2B product — just an account several people share.
  • Named guest lists. Today anyone with the link can answer as often as they like. For a number you cater 380 covers against, you need per-family invite links and fixed seat allowances.
  • Reminders. The entire value rests on response rate. Automatic, 14 and 7 days out, by WhatsApp and email — this is the feature that carries the €199.
  • Legal compliance as a feature. Downloadable data-processing agreement, the venue's imprint and privacy notice on every published invitation, documented deletion periods. The moment a business publishes the page, German imprint law applies.
  • Print file with QR code. Turns the print shop down the road from a competitor into a distribution channel — it keeps selling cards and bundles your counting with them.

The figures in the example are calculated, not measured: 60 weddings, 380 guests, 8% buffer, €16 food cost. Replace them in the meeting with the numbers the owner says out loud — the arithmetic only convinces when it is his.
